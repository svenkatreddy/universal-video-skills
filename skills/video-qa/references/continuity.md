# Continuity Across Joins

A video is a sequence of promises: this shot follows that one, same world,
same people, moments apart. Every cut either keeps the promise or breaks it.
Check every join for the five continuities:

1. **Identity.** Same faces, same wardrobe, same products on both sides of
   the cut. Compare boundary frames, not memories.
2. **Geography.** Characters don't teleport. If she exits right, she enters
   from the left, unless the cut is a deliberate time/place jump, in which
   case the audience needs an establishing cue.
3. **Light.** Noon doesn't cut to midnight. Grade and light direction should
   match unless the story changed the time.
4. **Screen direction.** Movement keeps its direction across the cut.
   Reversals need motivation and usually a neutral buffer shot.
5. **Sound.** Room tone continuous under the cut. A hard audio cut over a
   soft visual cut (or vice versa) feels broken even when both halves are
   fine.

## The join review

For each join, pull the last frame of clip A and the first frame of clip B
side by side. Ask the five questions. It takes thirty seconds per join and
it's the highest-value review in the project.

## Fixing breaks

- **Regenerate the weaker side** with the Preserve slot naming exactly what
  must match the other side ("same olive bomber jacket as clip 2, same
  night-market background").
- **Bridge it.** A cutaway, an insert, or two frames of black can hide a
  multitude of sins. Editors have done this for a century.
- **Cut around it.** If the join won't hold, one of the two shots goes.
  Keeping a broken join poisons the whole sequence.
